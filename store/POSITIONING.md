# Product positioning and launch plan

## Intended purpose

Help people gather source material before rewriting a website: its existing copy, content hierarchy, and relevant image references.

This purpose follows the requested workflow and implemented features. The audience and commercial plan below are recommendations to validate, not claims of measured demand.

Primary audience: freelance copywriters and small content teams preparing website rewrites. They need an organized starting point instead of repeatedly copying page fragments into a brief.

Secondary audience: web designers and content strategists reviewing existing client sites before a redesign. Social descriptions, alt text, and page-level publishing signals provide supporting context.

Core job: “When I begin a website rewrite, help me collect the existing words and understand their structure so I can prepare the brief and write with context.”

## Positioning

- Name: **Upkopp — Website Content Research**.
- Tagline: **Collect source material. Write with context.**
- Promise: **Start your next website rewrite with the source.**
- Product evidence: original text, headings and paragraphs, a navigable page map, combined JSON, plain-text capture, and one-folder image ZIPs.
- Trust: local processing, no account, no analytics, and access requested for specific features.

Avoid “AI copywriter,” “complete scraper,” “full SEO suite,” “download every image,” “100% private,” guaranteed token savings, or unmeasured time-saving claims. Writing and AI analysis happen elsewhere.

## Business recommendation

Make 1.0 free to use, with Upkopp branding and no login. Its initial business value is awareness and trust among people who may need Upkopp's content or website services. This is a hypothesis, not a revenue forecast.

Add the real Upkopp website and support contact before launch. Helpful guidance and clear publisher identity are enough for version one. Keep mandatory email capture, upsells, rating pressure, and tracking out of the research workflow.

Retain code ownership as requested. A public GitHub repository supports inspectability and bug reporting; it is not open source without a reuse license.

Consider paid offerings only after repeated requests identify a job worth paying for. Team research libraries or client handoff tools could be evaluated later; neither is promised or included in 1.0.

## Launch sequence

1. Complete native Chrome verification and public support/privacy setup in docs/RELEASE.md.
2. Invite 5–10 target users through channels you already manage. Ask them to prepare a brief for one public page. Sending invitations is a separate owner action.
3. Observe whether they reach a useful first export without explanation, where they get confused, and which fields they use. Do not request private client content.
4. Fix repeat blockers, submit for review, and publish the GitHub repository when ready.
5. After approval, demonstrate one real research workflow on Upkopp's website and communities that allow product posts, using the approved listing URL and actual screenshots.

## Measures

During the pilot, record manually: useful first exports completed, abandonment reasons, unwanted text in exports, image-download failures, repeat use reported a week later, and unresolved support issues. Five of ten people completing a useful export without assistance is a proposed early learning threshold, not an industry benchmark.

After launch, use store-provided installation trends and voluntary feedback. No extension analytics is implemented. Telemetry would require a separate decision and updated disclosures.
